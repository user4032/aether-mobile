01 How does encryption work in Lumyn?
We use X25519-based protocol (Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman) for key exchange and AES-256-GCM for symmetric encryption of every message. Keys are stored exclusively on your device.
02 Are my messages stored on the server?
The server acts only as a router. Once a message is delivered to the recipient, it is deleted from memory. If you use "Ephemeral Messages", they disappear from devices after being viewed.
03 Is the app supported on iPhone?
Yes, Lumyn works as a Progressive Web App (PWA). Simply open the site in Safari, tap Share, and select "Add to Home Screen".
04 Can I restore my chats on a new phone?
For maximum security (Zero-Knowledge Architecture), chat history is tied to cryptographic keys on your specific device. Cross-device synchronization is currently in development.
05 Is a phone number required to register?
No. Unlike Signal or Telegram, LUMYN is not tied to your phone number or SIM card. Your identity is your cryptographic key, generated locally on your device.
06 Where are my encryption keys stored?
Private Keys never leave your device. They are stored in your phone's secure local storage. The LUMYN server only sees public keys, which are sufficient for routing but cannot be used for decryption.
07 What if I lose my phone?
Since we use Zero-Knowledge architecture, private keys are stored exclusively on your current device and never reach the server. If you lose your device without a saved backup, recovering old conversations will not be possible. This is a conscious trade-off for absolute security.
08 Is LUMYN open-source?
Transparency is the foundation of trust in any cryptography. We plan to open-source the client apps on GitHub soon so any developer or auditor can verify the security of our encryption protocols.
09 Who is behind LUMYN's development?
LUMYN is an independent Ukrainian project. It's created by a single developer from Khmelnytsky.

The goal is simple: prove that true privacy, minimalist design, and Zero-Knowledge architecture don't have to belong to mega-corporations with billion-dollar budgets. Security should be fast, transparent, and accessible to everyone.
